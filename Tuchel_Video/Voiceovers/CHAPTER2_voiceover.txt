When the September international break came around, Tuchel made his first big moves. He didn't just tinker — he rebuilt.

He brought in players who would run. Players who would press. Players who would sacrifice their individual brilliance for the collective. Dan Burn. Elliot Anderson. Morgan Rogers. Names that made people laugh on social media.

But here's what people missed: Tuchel wasn't picking the best 26 players. He was picking the best team. And there's a massive difference.

He said it himself: "From day one we were clear we were trying to build the best possible team, which is not necessarily the 26 most talented players."

Palmer missed those September and October camps entirely. Foden was there in November, but Tuchel experimented with him in different roles — and what he saw clearly didn't convince him.

Meanwhile, the players who were there — the ones who bought into the system, who pressed like their lives depended on it, who celebrated together — they were building something. A brotherhood, as Tuchel called it.

And Palmer and Foden? They were on the outside looking in.
